# Intersection with a certified axis-aligned box — 2026-11-12

The user approved a limited experiment: use a temporary analytic box as an Intersection operand only when the source geometry and topology can be strictly certified; otherwise retain the existing path. This is not a general B-rep repair.

The checked solid Intersection path now examines each source. Its certificate requires one valid solid and shell, exactly six faces, 12 edges and eight vertices, uniform finite topology tolerance between OCCT confusion and 1e-6 mm, and strictly positive dimensions. It checks all eight exact axis-aligned corners, unique straight degree-one spline edges and their endpoints, six rectangular bilinear spline faces and their control nets, four distinct boundary edges per face, face adjacency, and linear face trims against the three-dimensional edge endpoints. It rejects seams, degenerate edges, nonuniform tolerances, unexpected topology and unsupported representations. Comparisons of the defining coordinates are exact; the certificate does not round a near-box into a box. A fresh analytic box with the same corners and topology tolerance is used only for the Boolean operand. If certification or construction cannot be established, the original operand is used. The document source is not rewritten. OCCT owns these geometry and topology checks in the native adapter; Rust retains the document and transaction.

The production certificate was exercised by `implementation/design/M01/viewport-export-separation-20261102/axis-box-certificate-probe.cpp`. The reopened, scaled spline box was accepted. Ten negative categories were rejected: an ordinary analytic box, cylinder, wedge, sheared box, sheared wedge, a box with a 1e-9 control-pole change, one with a 1e-9 trim change, mixed tolerances, reversed solid and cut box. The two tiny geometric changes remained valid according to OCCT's validity analyzer. Rejection means the old Boolean path remains in use, not that such a shape is invalid.

For the rotated radius-3 sphere and certified box, the checked kernel tests cover both operand orders and two profiles; the centered result has the analytic hemisphere volume and the offset result has the analytic spherical-cap volume. A near-centered sweep of 24 combinations accepted 16 correct-volume results and safely refused eight; it did not solve every case. Two separate truly disjoint fixture types remain empty. The previous empty, outside-input and missing-interior guards remain in place; bounded numerical witness searches do not prove all accepted results correct.

Controller fixtures exercise reopened sources, both profiles, retained source objects, correct analytic volume and area, saving and reopening the result, and Undo/Redo. Boolean Two additionally checks preview, deleting inputs, and Undo/Redo. An independent diagnostic read the actual controller-produced B-rep and classified 6,466 points analytically inside and 6,466 outside, all as expected; this is sampled evidence using OCCT classification, not a proof of every point or an independent geometry kernel.

This work does not certify arbitrary boxes, sheared solids, curved shapes, all Intersections, desktop behavior, or peak native memory. The temporary box is geometrically bounded, but OCCT allocations and transient native peak were not measured. Earlier findings in [the missing-witness record](2026-11-12-intersection-missing-witness.md) describe behavior before this narrowly certified path was introduced.

With the pinned OCCT 8.0.1 SDK, the kernel Boolean suite passed 91 tests with three opt-in or known-issue tests ignored, and the Solid controller regression group passed 95 tests. The Solid integration and workflow suites passed; the latter ran 114 tests with one native-UI fixture ignored. The public source audit reported no errors. These tests do not replace desktop or peak-memory validation.

## Near-centered refusal diagnosis

The eight refused combinations in the 24-case sweep use sphere centers at x = ±0.000001 mm, both profiles and both operand orders. At negative offset the guard detects a false empty result; at positive offset the native validity check reports a small edge. A separate opt-in diagnostic compared five equivalent sphere orientations, five offsets (−0.001, −0.000001, 0, 0.000001, 0.001 mm) and two profiles with the certified box. All ten y-axis 90° combinations produced the expected volume; at the two smallest offsets each of the other four orientations was refused for both profiles. The other offsets succeeded in this sample. This supports a representation or orientation sensitivity in this fixture, but does not establish its internal OCCT cause or prove that arbitrarily rotating a source preserves its geometry and topology. No sphere reconstruction, tolerance relaxation or change to rejection behavior was introduced. The 50-combination diagnostic and the 24-combination checked sweep passed; the former covers only one operand order and volume, not output equivalence or desktop behavior.

### Experimental sphere reconstruction — not integrated

The user approved an experiment with a temporary differently oriented sphere only after strict certification of the entire source. In an isolated OCCT probe, a sphere rebuilt with its polar axis along X and an analytic box yielded valid common results with the analytic spherical-cap volume for five offsets, including ±0.000001 mm, and both operand orders: 10/10. This is a native diagnostic with an analytic box, not a product result, saved/reopened input, Undo/Redo test, or a proof of result geometry beyond the validity and volume checks.

The experimental candidate predicate checks one valid forward solid, one spherical face, complete canonical UV bounds, one wire, two degenerate pole edges and a two-sided seam, and selected geometric constraints. It rejects the tested partial-longitude sphere, partial-latitude sphere, reversed solid, ellipsoid, cut sphere and 1e-9 shifted trim. The shifted trim remains valid according to OCCT's analyzer. A 1e-9 tilted seam and a 1e-9 rotation of the seam curve within its own plane were also OCCT-valid and initially accepted; additional frame checks now reject these examples. Moving one pole by 1e-9 was another OCCT-valid false acceptance; exact coordinate comparison rejects it, but also rejects four valid translated spheres because their pole coordinates differ from evaluated source values by as little as 5.1e-39 mm. An exact seam-endpoint comparison likewise rejected the unmodified OCCT sphere due to differences around 7.35e-16 mm. With exact pole checking, only the centered source and replacement were accepted, so only two of ten common cases ran and four translated source cases were rejected. The earlier 10/10 diagnostic used the preceding, insufficient predicate.

The candidate remains insufficient and must not be used as a product certificate. No replacement sphere or tolerance relaxation was added to the product; current safe refusals remain. Next investigate a rigorously justified numerical error bound and the complete seam and pole representations, including placement and round-trip behavior, with adversarial fixtures before considering integration. The test-only sources are `implementation/design/M01/viewport-export-separation-20261102/sphere-topology-probe.cpp` and `sphere-certificate-experiment.hxx`; `build-sphere-probe.py` builds into the existing target directory.

#### Canonical pole and serialization investigation

The diagnostic now compares source poles to poles generated by `BRepPrimAPI_MakeSphere` from the source analytic sphere's own center, radius and axes. The five directly created spheres at offsets −0.001, −0.000001, 0, 0.000001 and 0.001 mm all pass the candidate, the 1e-9 pole modification remains rejected, and 10 rotated-sphere common results (two operand orders) pass native validity and volume checks. The candidate also rejects the tested 1e-9 trim, seam tilt and seam phase modifications. This exact canonical-pole comparison does not establish equivalence of every B-rep field.

After an OCCT B-rep text write/read, all five valid spheres are rejected by the candidate: for example, the upper U bound changes from 6.2831853071795862 to 6.2831853071795898 and seam endpoint evaluation changes. Independently, reserializing each reopened sphere yielded the same bytes as serializing a newly reconstructed canonical sphere in these five fixtures; this is not a safe certificate on its own. In an adversarial case, moving an original pole by one representable double changes the in-memory geometry, remains OCCT-valid, and nevertheless produces exactly the same B-rep text. A point-by-point exact vertex write/read comparison detects this particular hidden modification and passes the five reopened fixtures, but does not cover all other geometry, trims, topology, flags or transforms. A 1e-9 change to only the 3D seam range was rejected and OCCT itself considered that fixture invalid. No product behavior changed. Further work needs a complete exact comparison against the round-tripped canonical representation, including all relevant geometry/topology fields and adversarial mutations, before testing integration; blindly comparing serialized bytes or loosening a tolerance would not satisfy the approved condition.

Another adversarial fixture starts from the reopened centered sphere and changes the seam circle radius by one representable double. OCCT still reports the shape valid and the vertex write/read comparison passes, although the seam geometry has changed; its serialized text differs in this fixture. This confirms that checking vertices alone is insufficient and that both field comparison and canonical representation matter.

#### Binary comparison and its limits

An isolated follow-up serialized shapes with OCCT 8.0.1 `BinTools` version 4 with triangulation disabled, and compared exact bytes against a canonical sphere passed through the same B-rep text write/read. All five previously tested reopened translated spheres matched; a source generated by the OM9 kernel with a 45° sphere rotation also matched. That kernel source was generated by the ignored diagnostic with the `3dm-brep` feature, not reopened as a whole OM9 document. Twelve tested geometry/topology mutations differed from a freshly constructed sphere, including the one-ULP pole and a topology flag; the one-ULP seam-radius mutation also differed. This remains bounded sample evidence rather than a proof that matching binary streams imply source equivalence.

Inspection of the pinned OCCT `BinTools_ShapeSet.cxx` found that version 4 does not write the cached UV endpoint fields for curves on surfaces. An independent fresh sphere with a 1e-9 mutation of a non-closed edge's cached UV point remained OCCT-valid and produced identical version-4 binary bytes before and after the mutation. Version 3 detected that mutation, but it omits the **first** cached UV pair of a closed-surface seam: changing that pair by 1e-9 remained OCCT-valid and produced identical version-3 bytes. These are cached UV fields; the experiments do not establish whether changing them alters the true analytic sphere or a Boolean result. They do demonstrate that binary equality alone is not a complete identity check. A candidate would at least need to compare all relevant cached UV pairs explicitly, bound its temporary buffers and verify any other skipped OCCT representations and error handling; it must still prove the canonical source and replacement match under the approved condition. No binary-based certificate or sphere substitution was integrated into the application. The `l2_01_10` diagnostic requires `--features 3dm-brep` in this checkout; without it the standalone test link has unresolved 3DM bridge symbols.

The probe subsequently collected both cached UV endpoint pairs for every closed-surface seam and the pair for each other curve-on-surface representation. Exact comparison detected each of the two UV-only counterexamples above; the five native reopened spheres and the one rotated OM9-kernel sphere matched their respective text-round-tripped canonical references in both this comparison and version-4 mesh-free binary output. The collector is a diagnostic, allocates unbounded vectors, and has not established handling of every possible curve representation or nonfinite input. No product gate should rely on it until those points, serializer omissions, resource use and a strict equivalence argument are addressed. This checks source representation in six fixtures; it is not a saved/reopened OM9-document or Undo/Redo integration test.

#### Bounded output and representation preflight

The separate probe now tests fixed-size 8,192-byte output buffers for OCCT binary and B-rep text writes; a fresh sphere used 939 binary bytes and 891 text bytes, while the rotated OM9-kernel source used 939 and 1,082 respectively. The binary bytes match the unbounded stream for the fresh fixture. Deliberately limiting either output to 64 bytes causes the helper to return failure rather than accept truncated output. This bounds only the explicitly provided output buffers; it does not bound OCCT's internal allocation, initial source parsing, reference construction, the diagnostic's other streams/vectors, or process peak.

A diagnostic preflight caps topology occurrences before building maps and accepts only one forward solid with one face and wire, three unique edges, two unique vertices without additional point representations, exactly one 3D curve representation and one analytic spherical surface/line-trim representation per edge. It rejects the tested partial spheres, reversed sphere and nonuniformly scaled sphere, while accepting the five B-rep-text reopened spheres and the rotated OM9-kernel source. As a further counterexample to binary identity, appending a null 3D-curve representation leaves the binary bytes unchanged; preflight rejects that altered source. This preflight is a filter, not an equivalence certificate: unhandled internal representations, allocator failures and complete equivalence remain to be established. It is not used by the product, and full saved-OM9-document, Undo/Redo and memory-pressure tests remain open.

The preflight was then extended in the test-only probe to inspect finite center, radius, selected tolerances, vertices, curve ranges, UV endpoint caches and location transforms, and to reject location chains longer than eight links. Direct checks accepted a constructed eight-link chain and rejected nine links; a NaN cached UV point was rejected. The five reopened native sphere fixtures and the rotated OM9-kernel fixture still pass. These checks do not establish finiteness of every nested geometry value or bound allocations performed by OCCT during initial parsing, topology traversal, location composition or serialization. Their limits and behavior are experimental, not a new product contract.

#### Near-centered OM9 kernel sources

The diagnostic now combines its structural preflight, finite checks, fixed-size binary/text outputs, exact mesh-free binary equality and explicit comparison of cached UV endpoint pairs and the range of each null 3D-curve representation. The candidate compares against two separately constructed, text-round-tripped reference spheres: a directly constructed sphere and a sphere constructed at the origin, text-round-tripped, translated through `BRepBuilderAPI_Transform`, and text-round-tripped again. This second path was needed for the OM9 kernel's untranslated-orientation sphere at x = ±0.000001 mm: its pole y-coordinate differs from the directly centered canonical reconstruction by approximately 5.2e-38 mm. The alternative matches exactly at the encoded representation level; no numeric tolerance was applied.

Four source B-reps generated through the actual OM9 kernel diagnostic—centers x = ±0.000001 mm, each with identity or 45° X-axis rotation—passed the experimental candidate. For each, a fixed-axis temporary sphere and the previously certified spline box gave an OCCT-valid Intersection with the analytic volume in both operand orders: eight successful native operations. The original source binary geometry and captured UV caches compared equal before and after those operations. For each of the four input sources, separate one-ULP pole or UV mutations and an added null 3D-curve representation were rejected by the candidate; additional isolated cases demonstrate that both the UV and null-curve-range changes can be invisible to binary serialization alone. These are four kernel B-reps read directly by a standalone C++ probe, not `.om9` document reopen, controller, Undo/Redo or desktop evidence. The native operations checked validity and volume, not complete geometric equivalence. The probe's optional export path is `OM9_INTERSECTION_CERTIFICATE_DIR` in `near_centered_intersection_rotated_sphere_diagnostic`; this ignored diagnostic passed with `--features 3dm-brep`.

The two reference-construction recipes and OCCT serialization remain an experimental recognition method, not an established proof that their rounded, trimmed source represents exactly the same complete solid as a differently oriented newly constructed sphere. The byte comparator also does not itself prove which OCCT state can affect the Boolean, and internal allocation/error bounds are not established.
