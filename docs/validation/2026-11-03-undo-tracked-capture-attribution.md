# Undo attribution of tracked captures

Undo inventory excludes immutable document, B-rep source and resource allocations already owned by a live tracked capture. Lease clones share ownership; only the last handle's release changes that ownership. The older untracked Arc capture remains conservatively charged to history. The shared account and its change signal survive coordinator replacement.

Capture registration and final release increment a saturating generation. The Options statistics read it without taking the reservation lock and report a pending reconciliation if the cached inventory is stale. The next successful publication or Undo policy operation reconciles; release alone does not synchronously trim history. Inventory holds the account lock and rejects an inventory when a concurrent final drop changes its generation. Failed edits preserve Undo and Redo. A finished transaction scope releases its old world before publishing, so its historical ownership is not incorrectly excluded.

Red/green tests exercise a shared 1 MiB B-rep source, cloned leases, release, pending status, failed edit with real Redo, and reconciliation on successful edit; separate fixtures cover shared resource payloads and conservative legacy captures, scope completion, a drop during inventory, lock-free statistics, and replacement with matching generations. After the changes, 263 core unit, 23 atomic transaction, 9 scope, 8 Undo-memory and 3 memory-budget tests passed. App checks passed: 19 B-rep picking, 11 gesture, 52 STL export and 4 Undo settings tests. Public-source audit passed; artifact pruning removed no runs.

These checks do not prove full ownership accounting or an immediate cap after a capture drops. Other untracked owners, allocator overhead, native/GPU/cache allocations and scratch remain incomplete; scans and holding the account lock during inventory need performance measurement. The interactive 256 MiB limit and complete 60% RAM admission remain open. No new release or physical desktop verification was performed for this change.
