# Top11 reseamed trim parameterization and 3DM

An authored test showed that trimming across the original domain seam produced a two-segment polycurve with equal segment fractions, although the retained source intervals had unequal parameter lengths. Evaluating the resulting curve at an interior fraction therefore did not generally evaluate the same source fraction. When the two exact pieces are compatible clamped NURBS of the same degree and share the joint pole exactly, Rust now joins them into one NURBS and maps the knot intervals in the original fraction ratio. Reversed pieces are oriented by reversing poles, weights and knots; rational weights are scaled uniformly across the second piece. Allocation is checked against the existing output budgets.

The fallback remains the prior multi-segment representation when the exact join conditions are not met; its composite fractions may differ from the source affine parameter. The constructed joint has degree-fold knot multiplicity, so derivative evaluation at that knot may still be limited by the evaluator's continuity policy even when the source is smoother. General multi-segment partial-trim parameterization and arbitrary extreme numerical cases are not established by this change.

Core regression covers polynomial and rational certified quadratic periodic NURBS, direct and nested, both directions, at seven fractions against the corresponding affine source fraction. A real native 3DM Trim fixture uses a rational nested reseamed curve, CMD and toolbar routes, and compares seven result fractions after import. Existing nested Split 3DM fixtures also pass. These fixtures do not establish external Rhino behavior or every representation.
