# Transaction publication after shared RAM changes

`commit_with_guard` previously revalidated retained admission before its callback only for a top-level Undo-recording transaction. A peer coordinator sharing the external-memory account could tighten the sampled RAM ceiling during that callback; the subsequent source/epoch check did not inspect the changed ceiling, and the candidate could be published while its held transaction/scene data exceeded the new limit. A red fixture reproduced publication for the recording case with a simulated 620 MiB held amount, a 1 GiB physical sample and a pre-existing Redo entry.

The commit path now checks the prepared reservation and retained-owner budget before the callback for both recording and non-recording transactions. After the callback, it acquires the external-capture and external-reservation locks in their existing order, rechecks the current constrained limit, held data, prepared peak and retained owners, then keeps the reservation lock through publication. A peer cannot modify that account's ceiling or reservations between this final check and publication. The new fixture passes for recording on and off, refusing the transaction and preserving the earlier Redo and snapshot. Another fixture adds an external reservation through a peer inside the callback and verifies refusal for both modes with its declared 750 MiB held amount.

The lock does not span the callback itself. Its scope includes publication and any synchronous Undo inventory/trim performed there; the added validation and lock duration have not been benchmarked on long histories or the desktop. Counters in the race fixtures are simulated; no hundreds-of-MiB allocation was made. A rejected transaction can have allocated Undo vector capacity before the callback, but its logical document, history and Redo remain unchanged.

`retained_usage_with_held_usage` previously read the shared ceiling before subtracting held bytes, then acquired the registry again to inventory owners. If another coordinator tightened the ceiling between those reads, the later minimum could apply the new ceiling to the already-reduced old limit rather than subtracting the held bytes from the new ceiling. The function now takes the existing capture-then-reservation lock order once, computes the constrained limit and subtracts held bytes under the same locks as the owner inventory. A boundary fixture checks that 420 MiB held plus 200 MiB reserved is rejected under a simulated 60% of 1 GiB, preserves the document and reservation, and succeeds after release. This fixture validates the combined accounting, but does not deterministically schedule the former race; the concurrency argument follows from the common lock scope. Lock duration and desktop cost remain unmeasured. Preparation as a whole still has no lifetime reservation and is not made race-free by this change.

The complete `om9-core` command passed on E: default-feature development build with `-j 2`, with two existing ignored scaling benchmarks and one pre-existing unused-mut warning. The 23 atomic transaction integration tests passed after the commit change. Application regression tests also passed: 9 gesture, 7 Gumball, and 1 background STL capture/reservation test. This establishes the tested commit path, not all entry points: preparation can still race with policy tightening before it reserves real scratch, other operations and GPU/native allocations remain outside the common account, the 256 MiB interactive ceiling remains, and an unsampled request does not test current free RAM. Do not infer a process-RSS bound, full 60% session enforcement or desktop latency from these tests.

## Opening a document while another owner reserves memory

`TransactionCoordinator::open_document` previously performed its retained-owner and candidate-peak checks before building the new world, then published without rechecking the shared account. A deterministic test opens a document containing an actual 150,000-point cloud under a 16 MiB test limit; immediately before publication a peer sharing the account reserves a simulated 14 MiB. Before the fix, the open returned success despite the combined peak exceeding that limit. The fixture starts with a Redo entry and verifies the failed open preserves the complete coordinator snapshot and Redo; after dropping the reservation, an equivalent new document opens successfully and the existing Redo policy remains unchanged.

The open path now records the candidate/preparation increment over its initial retained inventory. After candidate construction, it reacquires the external-document and reservation locks in the existing order, inventories current retained owners under the current ceiling, adds that increment with checked arithmetic and keeps the locks through publication. The test passed after the fix. The 14 MiB reservation is accounting, not an actual 14 MiB allocation; the cloud itself is a real allocation. The guard is an internal deterministic test hook and the normal public entrypoint supplies a no-op. This is one coordinator open path; it does not establish the separate application New/Open replacement path as covered by this race test.

On E: with `-j 2`, 247 core unit, 23 atomic-transaction, 9 scope, 11 application document-contract, 9 document-window and 8 history-update tests passed. The additional inventory and time under shared locks are unbenchmarked. Candidate preparation is still not protected by a lifetime reservation, unsampled requests do not enforce available physical RAM, and other document entrypoints and native/GPU owners need audit. The hard 256 MiB interactive default and overall 60% policy remain unresolved; no process memory cap or desktop responsiveness is claimed.
