# Top11 Trim: repeated surface-region picks

The OCCT-backed Top11 Trim session accepts one Surface or BRep target and one or more curve cutters. It partitions in world space, uses exact distance to identify a unique region, and publishes each removal as its own geometry transaction. The retained geometry is a BRep in the original target's local placement. Removing the last region deletes the target. The original cutters remain in the document. A native region-removal adapter emits a valid compound of remaining exact faces, allowing disconnected regions without incorrectly placing them in one shell; Rust retains selection, ownership, budgets and transaction control.

Controller tests split a 10 × 20 mm degree-1 NURBS surface with two crossing lines into four regions, remove all four including diagonally separated remnants, and undo/redo four transactions. A boundary pick is rejected without mutation. A BRep face cut by a circle retains an exact hole with area 200 − 4π mm²; a separately placed line/face case covers CMD selection, toolbar invocation and Undo/Redo; missed and canceled cuts leave no transaction. A simulated egui viewport test clicks two regions of a planar BRep and undoes both. This is not a physical desktop/GPU acceptance test.
