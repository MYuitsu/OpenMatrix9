# Top11 Trim: mixed curve and surface edits

One single-face surface/BRep and curve cutters now share an active Trim session: intersecting curves and divided surface regions can both be picked. Before a curve mutation is committed, Rust constructs the prospective curve replacements, recomputes intersection fractions, and asks OCCT to repartition the current committed surface using those replacements. A failed recomputation leaves the document and active state unchanged. A face removal similarly recomputes curve intersections before its transaction. A multi-face BRep remains unsupported for curve edits because removing obsolete internal boundaries could alter authored topology.

Fixtures exercise a planar typed NURBS surface and planar BRep, each with two crossing line cutters, in both edit orders. They check the expected 50 or 100 mm² remainder, rejection of ambiguous typed picks without a transaction, and complete Undo/Redo. The test removes part of a cutter after a surface region was removed and confirms the next surface pick uses the newly combined region. An egui simulated viewport fixture selects a curve over its face, then a surface region, and undoes both. The viewport gives a curve priority over a face at the same depth within document tolerance; a closer face still wins. A Rust fixture rejects sampled potential partial curve/surface overlaps rather than turning them into isolated hits.

Windows MSVC, pinned OCCT 8.0.1 runtime and `CARGO_TARGET_DIR=L:\om9-target`: app Top11/options/BRep passed 90 tests; core curve/surface/Top11 passed 46 tests; no-default Top11/options passed 65 tests; two simulated viewport fixtures passed; public source audit reported zero errors. This does not establish general mixed Trim: several surface/BRep targets and surface/BRep cutters, multi-face targets with curve edits, general curved and periodic topology, robust numerical tangencies/partial overlaps, .om9/3DM round trips, performance and desktop UI remain open. The overlap detector is conservative and sampling-based, not a proof of overlap classification. An unsplit remainder is not offered as a surface region until a cutter actually partitions it.
